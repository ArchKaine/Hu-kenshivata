# Hu-kenshivata
A constructed language for human communication.

## Hu-kenshivata: A Language for Connection
Welcome!

Hu-kenshivata (pronounced Hoo-ken-shih-vah-tah) is a constructed language designed to bridge communication and understanding between people from all walks of life. It prioritizes clarity, conciseness, and the ability to express complex ideas in a straightforward manner.

## Core Concepts
Hu-kenshivata is designed to bridge communication and understanding across cultures, with a strong emphasis on clarity and eliminating ambiguity. Here's what drives its development:

* **Global Communication:** Hu-kenshivata aims to be a neutral language, easily learned by people from diverse backgrounds. This can foster communication and collaboration on a global scale.

* **Clarity and Conciseness:** The language prioritizes clear and concise expression, minimizing the risk of misunderstandings. Grammatical structures and vocabulary choices are designed to ensure messages are effectively conveyed.

* **Reduced Ambiguity:** Hu-kenshivata is built with the goal of minimizing opportunities for misinterpretation. This is achieved through a focus on:
    * **Precise vocabulary:** Words are chosen to have distinct meanings, reducing the chance of confusion.
    * **Logical grammar:** Sentence structure is clear and predictable, preventing ambiguity in meaning based on word order.

* **Accessibility:** Hu-kenshivata strives to be user-friendly, with a base-10 number system and a logical grammatical structure. This makes it approachable for beginners, while still maintaining its focus on clarity.

* **Evolution and Community:** Hu-kenshivata is a work in progress, open to contributions from the language learning community. As the language evolves, feedback from users helps identify and address any potential ambiguities that might arise.

## Why Hu-kenshivata? A Comparison
Hu-kenshivata shares the goal of Lojban to create a universal language for clear communication. However, there are some key differences in design philosophy that might make Hu-kenshivata a better fit for some learners:

* **Focus on Accessibility:** Hu-kenshivata prioritizes user-friendliness. It utilizes a base-10 number system and a more natural grammatical structure for speakers of common languages. This can make it easier to learn and remember compared to Lojban's more complex system.

* **Reduced Ambiguity:** Hu-kenshivata is built with an emphasis on minimizing opportunities for misinterpretation. This is achieved through clear vocabulary choices and a logical sentence structure. Lojban, while powerful, can have a steeper learning curve due to its focus on efficiency and compactness, which can sometimes lead to ambiguity for beginners.

* **Evolution and Community:** Hu-kenshivata is a work in progress that welcomes community contributions. As the language evolves, user feedback helps identify and address any potential ambiguities or complexities. Lojban, while well-established, may have a more static structure, potentially limiting its ability to adapt to user needs.

Ultimately, the best language for you depends on your individual learning style and goals.

## Grammar
Hu-kenshivata's grammar is designed for logical consistency and clarity. Here is a glimpse into its structure:

* **Subject-Object-Verb Word Order:** Sentences typically follow an SOV order.
    * *Example:* `Mi datu kenita.` (I data understand.)

* **Rich Affix System:** Prefixes and suffixes are used to modify word meanings precisely.
    * *Prefixes:* `Na-` negates a word (`Na-bon` - Not good).
    * *Suffixes:* `-an` denotes a profession (`Tekmiran` - Engineer), and `-li` forms an adverb (`lenli` - slowly).

* **Simple Verb Tenses:** Verbs are conjugated for time with simple suffixes.
    * *Past:* `-le` (`chenale` - traveled)
    * *Present:* `-ta` (`chenata` - travels)
    * *Future:* `-shi` (`chenashi` - will travel)

* **Nuanced Expression (Modality):** Helper words (`pote` - can/might, `debe` - must/should) precede a verb to express possibility or obligation.
    * *Example:* `Nos debe ope.` (We must operate.)

* **Complex Sentences:** A set of conjunctions allows for fluid and logical compound sentences.
    * `Se... tunc...` (If... then...)
    * `Quam...` (Although...)
    * *Example:* `Se nos ope, tunc nos pote chena.` (If we operate, then we can travel.)

**Hu-kenshivata:**
> `Quam nos Loka-manye zun, nos pote wali ka u voko. Se nos wina-kenshita, tunc nos debe ope pro veza.`

**Pronunciation:**
> (Kwam nos Loh-kah-mahn-yay zoon, nos poh-tay wah-lee kah oo voh-koh. Say nos wee-nah-ken-shee-tah, tunk nos day-bay oh-pay pro vay-zah.)
